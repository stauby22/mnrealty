Agent’s description

Lakefront-adjacent, with a tailings view

Presenting the NorthMet Estate: an open-pit copper-nickel mine proposed near Babbitt and Hoyt Lakes, at the headwaters of the St. Louis River, which flows through the Fond du Lac Reservation and into Lake Superior. The developer, formerly PolyMet, is now NewRange Copper Nickel, a 50–50 joint venture between PolyMet — owned by the Swiss commodities giant Glencore — and Canada’s Teck Resources.

In May 2022, Glencore pleaded guilty in U.S. federal court to foreign bribery and market-manipulation schemes, resolving the charges for more than $1.1 billion. We consider this a strong credit reference.

In June 2023, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers revoked the mine’s Clean Water Act wetlands permit because it could not ensure compliance with the downstream Fond du Lac Band’s water-quality standards, which are stricter than Minnesota’s on mercury to protect fish and wild rice. Agent Stauber’s response: “The Biden Administration continues their assault on northern Minnesota and our way of life.”

In August 2026, NewRange applied again, with a redesigned tailings plan and about 1,100 acres of wetlands affected. Eight months earlier, Agent Stauber voted for the PERMIT Act, which narrows what states and authorized tribes can require in Clean Water Act certifications and limits how long the public has to sue (Roll Call 330).

Seller’s Disclosure Statement

Form MN-08-H2O · Watershed addendum
  1. Why was the first permit revoked?Because the Fond du Lac Band, downstream on the St. Louis River, has federally approved water-quality standards stricter than Minnesota’s, particularly for mercury. The Army Corps concluded the permit could not ensure those standards would be met.
  2. Who owns the buyer?NewRange is split 50–50 between PolyMet, owned by Glencore, and Teck Resources. Glencore pleaded guilty in 2022 to U.S. bribery and market-manipulation charges.
  3. What did the agent’s Clean Water Act vote do?The PERMIT Act limits state and tribal water-quality certifications to the discharge itself, gives the public 60 days to sue, and tells courts to generally leave permits in place while agencies fix defects. It passed the House; it has not become law.
  4. Correction to our earlier listingEarlier versions of this page estimated a specific percentage of “Russian ownership” through Glencore’s past stake in En+ Group. That figure could not be verified, and we have removed it. The guilty plea is on the record; we’ll stick with that.
