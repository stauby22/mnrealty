Agent’s description

Transparency, available once it was unanimous

Some properties sell themselves. This one had to be dragged onto the market. In 2025, a bipartisan discharge petition led by Reps. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) and Ro Khanna (D-Calif.) sought to force a House vote on releasing the Justice Department’s Epstein files over the objections of leadership. It needed 218 signatures. Four Republicans signed. Listing agent Pete Stauber was not one of them.

The petition reached 218 anyway. On November 16, 2025, the President reversed course and told House Republicans to vote yes. On November 18, the Epstein Files Transparency Act passed 427–1. Agent Stauber voted with the 427. We would describe his position as “firmly in favor of transparency, beginning two days after it became unanimous.”

The Justice Department then missed the law’s December 19 deadline, released files in batches, redacted pages the law said could not be redacted for embarrassment, and at one point declared its obligations fulfilled. As of September 2026, Rep. Massie says the government “continues to withhold over three million files,” and he has opened a second discharge petition to let survivors and state officials take the Justice Department to court.

Tenants interested in the property may wish to ask the agent a simple question: will he sign this one?
