⚠️ Property Disclosure: Constitutional Fine Print

Article I, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution grants Congress — not the President — the power "To lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises" and "To regulate Commerce with foreign Nations." Tariffs are, by constitutional design, a legislative power. By voting to block Congress from exercising this authority, Agent Stauber effectively endorsed handing the power of the purse to the executive branch. Both the Court of International Trade and the Federal Circuit have ruled unanimously that the President's use of IEEPA to impose tariffs is unlawful. Agent Stauber would apparently prefer Congress not weigh in at all.

Editor’s update: Ten days after this vote, on Feb. 20, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that the emergency-powers law does not authorize the tariffs. The day after this failed rule, Agent Stauber voted against H.J. Res. 72 to end the Canada tariff emergency (Roll Call 65); it passed 219–211 anyway.
