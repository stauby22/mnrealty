Agent’s description

He said “no charges dropped.” They dropped all of them.

Agent Stauber spent 23 years with the Duluth Police Department, by his own count, and he rarely lets a listing go by without mentioning it. So in January 2021, the month a mob broke through police lines at the Capitol, he said the right thing: “I want everybody that assaulted any police officer, or did any damage, or tried to interrupt the electoral count, I want each and every one of them accountable and held to the highest standard of the law and no charges dropped.”

Then the property went on the market. In May 2021 he voted against an independent, bipartisan commission to investigate the attack (Roll Call 154) and, the next day, against $1.9 billion to harden the Capitol and repay the police agencies that defended it — a bill that passed by one vote (Roll Call 156). That July, as officers testified to the House about what was done to them, his office told the Duluth News Tribune: “Unfortunately, we will not be able to accommodate this request.”

On January 20, 2025, the new owner pardoned roughly 1,500 January 6 defendants and commuted the sentences of 14 more, including members of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers convicted of seditious conspiracy. Just Security counted about 608 who had been charged with assaulting, resisting or impeding officers and 172 who had pleaded guilty to assaulting law enforcement.

We searched for the agent’s statement on the pardons. We could not find one. He does have views on clemency: in July 2026 he joined Minnesota’s Republican House delegation on a letter about a pardon granted by Minnesota’s own Board of Pardons. Some pardons, apparently, are a matter of public safety. Others are a private sale.
