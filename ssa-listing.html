Agent’s description

A guaranteed income stream, now with a maturity date

No asset in the 8th District pays out like this one. In December 2025, 197,404 district residents — about 27% of the population — collected Social Security, including 156,986 retired workers and 19,525 disabled workers. Together they received $378.5 million every month. That money buys groceries in Hibbing, pays property taxes in Aitkin, and keeps the lights on in Grand Rapids.

The June 2026 trustees’ report moved the retirement trust fund’s depletion date up a year, to 2032. If Congress does nothing, benefits are cut automatically by about 22%. The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget translated that for a typical couple retiring in 2033: $18,400 a year.

Part of the deterioration is a known renovation. The One Big Beautiful Bill, which Agent Stauber voted for twice, reduced the revenue the trust funds collect from taxing benefits — one of the reasons the trustees cited for the worse outlook (2026 Trustees Report). Meanwhile the agency answering the phones has shed staff: Social Security’s workforce is the smallest since 1967, and Minnesota’s SSA staff is down 13%.

Agent Stauber is a member of the Republican Study Committee, whose fiscal 2025 budget proposed “modest adjustments to the retirement age for future retirees.” We list that under “future renovations.”

Seller’s Disclosure Statement

Form MN-08-SSA · Retirement addendum
  1. Is Social Security “going broke”?No. Payroll taxes keep coming in. The trust fund reserves run out in 2032, after which incoming taxes cover about 78% of scheduled retirement benefits. Congress can prevent the cut; it has done so before.
  2. Did the agent’s tax law help or hurt?The trustees counted the One Big Beautiful Bill as one of the reasons the outlook worsened, because it reduces the income-tax revenue on benefits that flows back into the trust funds. The senior deduction in the law is also temporary, scheduled through 2028.
  3. Why do staffing cuts matter if my check still comes?Because new retirees, survivors and disability applicants still need a human to process a claim, fix an overpayment or change an address. Fewer staff means longer waits.
  4. Did the agent vote to lower seniors’ drug costs?No. He voted against the Inflation Reduction Act, which capped Medicare insulin at $35 a month and out-of-pocket Medicare drug costs at $2,000 a year.
