Agent’s description

Thank you for your service. Please hold.

The 8th District is home to 44,393 veterans — about one resident in thirteen. Their care runs through VA clinics in places like Ely and through the Minneapolis VA Health Care System, and their paperwork runs through a VA that has been shrinking.

Let’s start with the burn pits. In March 2022, the House passed the PACT Act, expanding care for veterans exposed to toxic burn pits, 256–174. Agent Stauber voted no (Roll Call 57). In July, the final version came back from the Senate with overwhelming support. He voted yes (Roll Call 309). In real estate, we call this rejecting the first offer and then showing up at closing for the photo.

Since 2025, the VA has cut about 30,000 positions through attrition and, in December 2025, moved to eliminate up to 37,000 more unfilled positions. The VA Inspector General found 130 severe occupational staffing shortages at the Minneapolis VA, 78 of them clinical. In September 2025, six members of Minnesota’s delegation wrote the VA Secretary demanding the state’s workforce data. Agent Stauber did not sign.

And in the One Big Beautiful Bill, which the agent voted for twice, veterans lost their exemption from SNAP work requirements. Minnesota expects thousands of people to lose food assistance under the new rules, veterans among them.

In memoriam

Alex Pretti was a 37-year-old intensive-care nurse at the Minneapolis VA — one of the people who care for this district’s veterans when they are sickest. On January 24, 2026, during the federal immigration surge in Minneapolis, he was pepper-sprayed, tackled and shot by federal agents. Multiple news organizations that reviewed the video found he was holding a phone, not a gun. The administration said he intended to “massacre law enforcement.”

The next day, Agent Stauber called it a “tragedy” and said it was “past time for deescalation” — and blamed Twin Cities politicians for “urging people to interfere” with law enforcement. The FBI then refused to share its evidence with Minnesota investigators, and the state sued.
